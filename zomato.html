01

Overview

THE DESIGN INTENT

Intent before inventory.

The Zomato proposal begins with what someone wants to do. The navigation and content then follow that choice.

This project rethinks navigation and the core ordering journey. Its outputs are an information architecture and user flow, rather than high-fidelity app screens.

Explore the Figma file ↗

02

Problem

Too many destinations. Too little direction.

In the version I audited, food delivery, dining, grocery and events competed for attention. Cuisine shortcuts, promotional banners and restaurant cards added more choices before a person could act on their intent.

03

Process

01

Audit the structure

I mapped the existing verticals and identified competing navigation, content overload, unclear promotional hierarchy and redirects to separate services.

02

Group by intent

Card sorting and tree-testing methods informed the proposed structure. The case-study materials do not record a participant count or quantitative validation results.

03

Reveal information in context

Progressive disclosure shapes Home and the ordering journey: show the choices relevant to the task, then introduce detail when it is needed.

04

Key decisions

DECISION / 01

Keep four stable destinations

Home, Search, Orders and Account become fixed tabs. Blinkit and District remain reachable through deep links outside the core navigation.

DECISION / 02

Ask for intent before content

An Order in / Dine out toggle makes Home relevant to the immediate task. Six cuisine shortcuts and See all reduce the initial choice set.

DECISION / 03

Bring browsing and search together

People who know what they want search; others browse. Both routes converge on one restaurant page, followed by a consistent cart and checkout.
