01

Overview

THE DESIGN INTENT

Leave room for uncertainty.

Verge makes space for personal priorities and hesitation. A useful reflection should help someone think, without deciding for them.

Verge helps someone name a decision, rank what matters, reflect on their feelings and assess readiness. The concept explores a career decision about relocating for a new role.

Explore the Figma file ↗

02

Problem

Not every reason belongs on the same side of a list.

A pros-and-cons list treats every reason as equal. Difficult decisions need room for personal priorities, uncertainty and the possibility that someone is not ready yet.

03

Process

01

Name the decision

A simple first step asks what someone is weighing, with a category and optional context.

02

Find the priorities

Choose up to three priorities, identify worries and put the priorities in order.

03

Reflect, then review

Separate excitement from hesitation before reviewing the suggested direction and setting a personal readiness score.

04

Key decisions

DECISION / 01

Name it before solving it

The first screen reassures the user: No need to solve it yet. Optional context lowers the pressure to write a perfect answer.

DECISION / 02

Make the trade-off visible

Choosing three priorities from six, then ranking them, lets the review connect back to what this person values most.

DECISION / 03

Leave room for Not yet

The review proposes a direction but includes This feels right and Not yet, plus a readiness slider. Testing must establish whether its interpretation feels helpful or too prescriptive.

05

Final screens

06 / WHAT I’D DO NEXT

The next useful question.

Design the Not yet path and the follow-up after saving a decision. Test with people facing a real choice, especially whether the generated reflection overstates what their words imply.
