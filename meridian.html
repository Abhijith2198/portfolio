Overview

THE DESIGN INTENT

The journey starts with trust.

Meridian balances the feeling of a place with the practical next step: an inquiry shaped around the traveller.

Meridian is a fictional travel company built around regional specialists and bespoke itineraries. I designed the desktop homepage, Journal index, article page and a mobile homepage.

Explore the Figma file ↗

02

Problem

Sell a sense of possibility, then make the next step obvious.

A catalogue of packages would work against Meridian's personal service. The challenge was to communicate place and expertise while keeping one action clear: starting a trip inquiry.

03

Process

01

Define the visual world

Spruce, stone and brass colors pair with Instrument Serif and Hanken Grotesk. Coordinates and route markings make the travel identity specific.

02

Build a reusable system

I assembled the homepage with auto layout and reusable components, then extended the system into the Journal index and article page.

03

Refine with feedback

Mentor feedback led to a wider inquiry bar, a clearer way to view all journeys and a route motif in the hero. The homepage was then adapted to 440 px mobile.

04

Key decisions

DECISION / 01

Give the inquiry room to work

The inquiry bar grew from 720 px to the full 1200 px content width. Adding Trip style captures how someone wants to travel alongside where, when and with whom.

DECISION / 02

Make the route the signature

A dashed route with waypoints replaced less effective hero treatments. A mask and Smart Animate create its reveal in the Figma prototype.

DECISION / 03

Carry one idea across the system

Coordinates connect the latitude strip, destination photography and footer. The same editorial hierarchy carries through the Journal and its stories.

05

Final screens

Travel photography is used within this fictional design concept.

The next useful question.

Complete the mobile Journal pages and design the trip-inquiry flow. Test whether travellers naturally begin with the inquiry bar or explore signature routes first.
